# Methods: Discovery of emergent visual demand dimensions

**Motivation.** The ADeLe rubric battery (Zhou et al., 2026) was developed on text-only tasks. A first-pass scoring of six ADeLe scales on the SciVer science reasoning benchmark (Wang et al., 2025) showed all six were nearly flat (s.d. < 1.0 across items), indicating the existing battery does not capture demand difficulty in the context of figure-based claim verification. We therefore ran an inductive discovery procedure to identify demand dimensions specific to figure-grounded tasks. All discovery used SciVer (Wang et al., 2025) and SciClaimEval (Ho et al., 2026); CharXiv was held out throughout.

**Stage 1 — Open annotation.** We employed Claude Opus 4.8 (Anthropic; model `claude-opus-4-8`; run June 12, 2026) as our LLM annotator. (Claude Fable 5 was the intended annotator, but the session was downgraded to Opus 4.8 early in the run; all 30 annotation batches were verified from transcripts to have run on Opus 4.8, so the resulting codebook is an Opus 4.8 artifact.) The annotator was given 300 discovery items (200 SciVer single-figure-evidence, 100 SciClaimEval figure-evidence; claim + figure) in 30 batches of 10, blind to the dataset verdict labels, and asked in each batch to name any demand features the current codebook failed to capture. The codebook was seeded with 23 sub-features carried in from ADeLe/DeLeAn and the Tolan et al. (2021) visual-processing branch, and each batch prompt embedded the codebook as accumulated so far, so proposals measure marginal novelty; the pass produced 89 new sub-features, for 112 total. Saturation was assessed by plotting cumulative unique features against items annotated: the discovery rate roughly halved over the first two hundred items (44 new features in items 1–100, 23 in items 101–200) but then plateaued rather than converged (22 in items 201–300), and the pre-registered stopping criterion (fewer than 2 new features over 50 consecutive items) was never met before the 300-item cap. The codebook is therefore not saturated at this sample size; consolidation (Stage 2) precedes any use.

**Stage 2 — Semantic grouping.** The sub-features were grouped by the LLM into 9 parent dimensions, with human review of the assignments (e.g., all features about finding the correct data point grouped under localization/grounding).

**Stage 3 — Spread test.** For each parent, we counted how many of its sub-features fired on each item, giving a per-item presence count (N = 300). A dimension scoring nearly the same everywhere cannot separate easy from hard items. Localization/grounding showed the strongest spread (μ = 5.54, σ = 2.59), followed by referent mismatch (σ = 2.41; flagged leakage-suspect in the discovery pass, as its scoring can correlate with the verdict) and figure legibility (σ = 2.03); multi-element reasoning (σ = 1.01) and gestalt/shape (σ = 0.90) were intermediate, and claim-side linguistic load was near-flat (σ = 0.73). Presence also varied: multi-element reasoning appeared in every item, while scope overreach appeared in only 43%.

**Stage 4 — Rubric drafting.** Three candidate dimensions — Visual Localization and Grounding (VL), Multi-Element Visual Aggregation (MA), and Gestalt and Shape Judgment (GS) — were selected on spread together with judgment about conceptual distinctness, and written as full ADeLe-style rubrics: a definition paragraph, a scope note, an explicit boundary discussion distinguishing each from its neighbors, a confound caveat, and anchored levels 0–5+ with examples. Rubrics were LLM-drafted from ADeLe appendix exemplars, then refined through human review, an initial revision pass removing overly specific levels, a second pass resolving ambiguity, and a final human pass. All three entered the final 12-dimension battery.
